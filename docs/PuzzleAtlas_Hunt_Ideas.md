# 1. How to use this catalogue

These concepts are deliberately developed beyond a title and theme. Each includes an investigation shape, mechanical identity and convergence. They are starting points for research, location scouting, puzzle design and testing in PuzzleAtlas Studio.

| **Portfolio rule  **A strong catalogue should vary not only its setting, but also how players think: observe, compare, route, negotiate, reconstruct, communicate, classify or form a theory. |
| --- |

## Selection criteria

A clear player role and motivating mystery.

At least two meaningful open leads during the central act.

A convergence that creates a deduction rather than merely concatenating answers.

A setting or evidence set that materially shapes the puzzles.

A feasible fallback for fragile real-world dependencies.

An ending that reinterprets what the player has learned.

## Reusable structures

| **Pattern** | **Shape** | **Best use** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Three independent lenses | Three branches reveal person, time and place | Classic investigation |
| Competing theories | Evidence supports and eliminates explanations | Detective or historical mystery |
| Distributed knowledge | Players receive different partial truths | Party and team play |
| Open district | Several sites available in any order | Location exploration |
| Escalating archive | Each solved dossier changes interpretation of earlier evidence | Armchair mystery |
| Choice and consequence | Branches affect allies, resources or ending | Replayable narrative |

# 2. Location-based hunt concepts

## The Printer’s Last Edition

**Format: **Location-based / 2–3 hours   **Setting: **Boston — Freedom Trail and adjacent historic streets   

A suppressed newspaper was printed on the night before a decisive political event. The player must reconstruct its final edition before a rival collector destroys the surviving evidence.

### Investigation shape

Printer branch identifies which phrases were altered between editions.

Courier branch traces how separate pages moved through the district.

Witness branch establishes the true order of nighttime events.

The branches converge into a reconstructed front page that reveals the final meeting place.

### Signature mechanics

Durable architectural observation, public inscriptions and street layout.

Layering page fragments and comparing contradictory headlines.

A route-choice problem framed as avoiding patrols rather than shortest-path navigation.

### Finale

The ‘treasonous’ edition is revealed as deliberate misinformation designed to protect a clandestine evacuation. Players decide whether the archive should publish the truth or preserve the deception.

| **Design note  **Avoid tying mandatory clues to plaques or displays that may be removed; every observation needs a verified alternative. |
| --- |

## The Twenty-Third Square

**Format: **Location-based / 2–3 hours   **Setting: **Savannah — Historic District   

A surveyor’s notebook claims Savannah was intended to contain a lost twenty-third square. Several families altered the city records to erase why it mattered.

### Investigation shape

Geometry lead studies the repeating ward plan and anomalies.

Botany lead interprets tree and garden symbolism across selected squares.

Civic lead reconstructs renamed or repurposed spaces from records supplied in-app.

The combined evidence locates a ‘square’ that exists as relationships between four sites, not a hidden park.

### Signature mechanics

Map overlay, spatial symmetry and route freedom.

Observation prompts based on durable layout rather than movable decoration.

Final triangulation using discoveries from all branches.

### Finale

Standing at the inferred centre reveals an AR-style historical overlay or conventional illustrated reveal: the lost square was a proposed refuge whose purpose survived in the surrounding institutions.

| **Design note  **The square grid naturally supports nonlinear play because branches can occupy distinct clusters and reconverge centrally. |
| --- |

## Ledger of the Two Harbours

**Format: **Location-based / 2.5–3.5 hours   **Setting: **Lower Manhattan   

An eighteenth-century shipping ledger contains transactions that should be impossible: cargo arriving before departure and merchants trading under names that did not yet exist.

### Investigation shape

Harbour branch reconstructs routes and tides from supplied charts.

Merchant branch identifies aliases through building symbols and street history.

Clock branch resolves conflicting calendars and recorded times.

Convergence reveals that the ledger encoded messages for an escape network.

### Signature mechanics

Evidence table, map bearings and chronological deduction.

Multiple plausible theories gradually eliminated.

Optional finance puzzle unlocks a richer ending but does not block completion.

### Finale

The player follows the decoded instruction to a waterfront sightline, where the final message reframes ‘cargo’ as people whose identities were protected.

| **Design note  **Sensitive history requires expert consultation and a respectful tone; fictional intrigue must not trivialise real suffering. |
| --- |

## The Architect’s Shadow

**Format: **Location-based / 2–3 hours   **Setting: **Chicago — The Loop and riverfront   

An architect hid a second plan of the city inside the alignments, reflections and ornament of later buildings. A developer wants it found before a public hearing.

### Investigation shape

Form branch classifies façades and recurring geometric motifs.

River branch uses bridge and street orientations to establish axes.

Reflection branch compares supplied images with live viewpoints.

The final overlay reveals which planned public space was never built.

### Signature mechanics

Sightlines, reflection matching and architectural pattern recognition.

A physical map that becomes more useful as translucent layers unlock.

Choice between assisting the developer, archivist or neighbourhood group.

### Finale

The hidden plan is not a treasure map but evidence that land was intended for public use. The ending varies according to whom the player trusted.

| **Design note  **Use broad public viewpoints; do not encourage players to stand in entrances or focus on screens near traffic. |
| --- |

## The Brass Compass

**Format: **Location-based / 2–3 hours   **Setting: **Old San Juan   

A navigator imprisoned within the fortified city designed a compass that points not north, but toward the weakest story in any official account.

### Investigation shape

Fortification lead studies walls, gates and defensive geometry.

Navigator lead decodes wind, star and bearing notes.

Testimony lead compares three narrators whose accounts cannot all be true.

Together they identify an overlooked route and the reason it was erased.

### Signature mechanics

Compass tasks with generous tolerance and non-sensor fallback.

Fortification map assembly and contradiction analysis.

Optional elevation-dependent viewpoints with accessible alternatives.

### Finale

The escape route proves to have been used to bring medicine into the city rather than flee it. The compass becomes a symbol for questioning authorised history.

| **Design note  **A strong setting, but climate, shade, surfaces and storm-season operations need explicit planning. |
| --- |

# 3. European location concepts

## The City Beneath the Closes

**Format: **Location-based / 2.5–3 hours   **Setting: **Edinburgh — Old Town   

A mapmaker catalogued paths that vanished as the city built upward. His final survey separates physical streets from remembered routes.

### Investigation shape

High City branch uses skyline, public stairs and visible landmarks.

Low City branch reconstructs lost routes from in-app archival fragments.

Voices branch compares accounts from residents at different social levels.

Convergence produces a vertical map and reveals the mapmaker’s final destination.

### Signature mechanics

Elevation, line-of-sight and layered maps.

Narrative clues that change meaning when viewed from another level.

Accessibility route that preserves deductions without requiring every stair.

### Finale

The missing route was erased intentionally to protect a network moving food during a crisis. The true map records relationships rather than streets.

| **Design note  **The concept must be designed around practical gradients and alternatives, not merely aesthetic stairways. |
| --- |

## The Astronomer’s Error

**Format: **Location-based / 2–3 hours   **Setting: **Prague — Old Town and Malá Strana   

A court astronomer appears to have made one impossible mistake in every surviving calculation. The errors may form a message—or expose a fraud.

### Investigation shape

Instrument branch interprets supplied diagrams against architectural motifs.

Calendar branch resolves dates recorded under different systems.

Patron branch traces who benefited from each ‘mistake’.

The corrected errors form coordinates only after the political motive is understood.

### Signature mechanics

Clock and calendar logic, symbol classification and map overlay.

Competing theories: incompetence, sabotage or deliberate protection.

An optional advanced cipher that confirms rather than supplies the main deduction.

### Finale

The astronomer altered predictions to prevent a purge prompted by a manufactured omen. The final celestial diagram becomes a record of saved lives.

| **Design note  **Avoid relying on crowded choke points; allow investigation branches to be completed in flexible order. |
| --- |

## Republic of Masks

**Format: **Location-based / 2.5–3.5 hours   **Setting: **Venice — San Marco fringe and quieter sestieri   

A diplomatic courier carried three identities and one message through the republic. Each identity left a different trail, and only one was genuine.

### Investigation shape

Official trail follows seals, civic symbols and formal routes.

Merchant trail follows trade signs, bridges and delivery timings.

Performer trail interprets masks, roles and staged testimony.

Players combine route feasibility with motive to identify the true courier.

### Signature mechanics

Bridge-constrained routing, identity logic and staged conversations.

Tidal or access information supplied in-app rather than assumed live.

A final choice about whether to deliver, expose or alter the message.

### Finale

The authentic identity belongs not to a spy but to a printer attempting to prevent a war sparked by forged correspondence.

| **Design note  **The route must be resilient to crowding, flooding and temporary bridge or alley restrictions. |
| --- |

## The Canal Cipher

**Format: **Location-based / 2–3 hours   **Setting: **Amsterdam — Canal Belt   

Four merchant houses used canal addresses, gable symbols and cargo codes to conceal a shared debt. One ledger page can reveal who betrayed the group.

### Investigation shape

House branch matches durable façade forms to ledger marks.

Water branch treats canal rings and bridges as a routing graph.

Cargo branch identifies substitutions in coded manifests.

The combined structure yields a name, motive and meeting bridge.

### Signature mechanics

Network paths, visual classification and substitution logic.

Several valid branch orders with a shared waterside convergence.

Evidence-board interface for linking house, cargo and merchant.

### Finale

The apparent betrayal was a coordinated bankruptcy designed to prevent a monopoly. Players choose which interpretation the final archive records.

| **Design note  **Keep players away from cycle lanes while observing façades; instructions must foreground safe stopping points. |
| --- |

# 4. Armchair hunt concepts

## The Atlas of Impossible Islands

**Format: **Armchair / 90–150 minutes   **Setting: **Fictional global archive   

A nineteenth-century atlas includes six islands that never existed, yet each appears in unrelated modern evidence. The player must discover why a cartographer invented them.

### Investigation shape

Map branch compares coastlines, scales and projection distortions.

Correspondence branch identifies coded relationships among sponsors.

Specimen branch tests whether labelled plants could share an origin.

The islands combine into a distributed warning about an impending financial fraud.

### Signature mechanics

Layered maps, document comparison, scientific classification and acrostic restraint.

Evidence can be pinned and linked on a caseboard.

Multiple theories remain viable until a final falsification step.

### Finale

The fake islands were designed as traceable errors: whichever patron copied them would expose the theft of the atlas plates.

| **Design note  **An ideal flagship armchair hunt because it expresses the Atlas identity without depending on real-time external websites. |
| --- |

## Frequency 47

**Format: **Armchair / 75–120 minutes   **Setting: **Late-1970s radio archive   

Recovered broadcasts from a closed numbers station contain weather, music and shipping notices. A missing technician may have embedded one final transmission.

### Investigation shape

Audio branch identifies rhythm, repetition and anomalies.

Personnel branch reconstructs shifts and access from memos.

Transmission branch maps frequencies and broadcast coverage.

The branches converge on which recording is authentic and what was smuggled through it.

### Signature mechanics

Audio spectrogram template with transcript alternative.

Timeline deduction and authenticity comparison.

Optional expert layer for radio enthusiasts, never required.

### Finale

The message is a warning about an industrial accident suppressed by both sides of a political divide, not conventional espionage.

| **Design note  **All audio-critical facts need captions and an equivalent visual route. |
| --- |

## Estate of the Last Illusionist

**Format: **Armchair / 90–180 minutes   **Setting: **Contemporary probate case   

An illusionist’s will distributes objects to five estranged apprentices, but every bequest seems insulting or impossible. Solving the estate reveals the final performance.

### Investigation shape

Workshop branch examines apparatus sketches and invoices.

Relationship branch reconstructs rivalries through letters and interviews.

Performance branch orders posters, reviews and stage cues.

Each bequest becomes a component in a collaborative mechanism.

### Signature mechanics

Inventory combination, perspective images and sequence reconstruction.

Player may accuse an apprentice early, but evidence can reopen the theory.

Emotional deductions matter alongside mechanical ones.

### Finale

The ‘inheritance’ assembles into a device that projects a final apology, leaving players to decide how the estate is divided.

| **Design note  **Keep illusions original or historically generic; do not reproduce proprietary routines. |
| --- |

## The Borough That Voted Twice

**Format: **Armchair / 90–150 minutes   **Setting: **Fictional municipal investigation   

A tiny borough recorded more ballots than residents in a century-old referendum. The obvious fraud explanation collapses when the census, map and minutes are compared.

### Investigation shape

Boundary branch traces annexations and address changes.

Register branch identifies duplicate names that may not be duplicate people.

Minutes branch analyses who controlled the meeting and its wording.

The final deduction depends on the legal definition of residency supplied within the hunt.

### Signature mechanics

Map boundaries, name disambiguation and procedural logic.

Competing hypotheses with explicit evidence for and against.

A final written conclusion scored against required findings.

### Finale

The second set of voters were workers housed on a temporary structure just outside the mapped boundary but inside the legal jurisdiction.

| **Design note  **Use a fully fictional jurisdiction to avoid false implications about real elections. |
| --- |

# 5. Party hunt concepts

## Museum After Midnight

**Format: **Party / cooperative / 4–10 players / 60–90 minutes   **Setting: **A fictional museum gala   

During a blackout, an artefact is replaced with a perfect copy. Players take staff and guest roles, each with incomplete access and potentially misleading assumptions.

### Investigation shape

Security team reconstructs movement and sensor events.

Curatorial team authenticates objects and catalogue records.

Guest team decodes invitations, gossip and social relationships.

Teams must share selected facts at scheduled briefings before the final accusation.

### Signature mechanics

Role-specific evidence, timed reveals and controlled information sharing.

One host device plus optional companion devices.

No player is secretly required to sabotage the group in the base version.

### Finale

The original was never stolen; the copy was installed years earlier, and the blackout exposes an institutional cover-up.

| **Design note  **Works well as an early multiplayer format because synchronization can occur at authored checkpoints. |
| --- |

## The Split Briefcase

**Format: **Party / cooperative / 3–8 players / 45–75 minutes   **Setting: **Contemporary courier thriller   

A courier delivers a locked briefcase whose mechanisms can only be solved by people holding different fragments of its instructions.

### Investigation shape

Players receive private diagrams, witness accounts and coded labels.

Small groups solve parallel mechanisms but cannot see the full state.

Two scheduled swaps redistribute information.

A final collaborative input requires simultaneous contributions.

### Signature mechanics

Distributed knowledge, verbal description and asymmetric interfaces.

Adaptive role balancing for different player counts.

Accessibility setting reduces time pressure and substitutes visual/audio information.

### Finale

Opening the case reveals evidence that the courier is one of the players’ future selves, followed by a final group choice about sending it onward.

| **Design note  **The puzzle must prevent one dominant player from collecting every device and solving alone. |
| --- |

## Council of the Last Lighthouse

**Format: **Party / negotiation and deduction / 5–12 players / 60–100 minutes   **Setting: **Storm-bound island council   

A failing lighthouse can guide only one of several approaching ships. Every player represents an island interest and holds different forecasts, histories and obligations.

### Investigation shape

Technical group evaluates weather and machinery evidence.

Archive group uncovers why the shipping records conflict.

Council group negotiates resources and public consequences.

A shared decision is made before the final evidence packet arrives.

### Signature mechanics

Private goals without simple traitor mechanics.

Negotiation changes which evidence becomes available.

Several defensible endings judged by reasoning, not a single moral score.

### Finale

The final packet reveals that the ships are coordinating a rescue; the best solution depends on whether players preserved enough trust and resources to recognize it.

| **Design note  **Designed for replayability: forecasts, interests and ship identities can vary by scenario. |

# 6. Recommended launch portfolio

The first public catalogue should prove the breadth of PuzzleAtlas without requiring every technically ambitious feature at once.

| **Priority** | **Hunt** | **Why it belongs** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The Atlas of Impossible Islands | Flagship brand fit; proves deep armchair play and core evidence tools |
| 2 | The Printer’s Last Edition | Clear location proposition in a naturally coherent walking district |
| 3 | Museum After Midnight | Demonstrates social play with manageable synchronization |
| 4 | The Twenty-Third Square | Shows nonlinear spatial investigation and a distinctive city identity |
| 5 | Estate of the Last Illusionist | Narrative-rich indoor experience with inventory and emotional payoff |
| 6 | The Architect’s Shadow | Adds choice, architecture and a modern urban tone |

## Mechanic coverage

Text, choice, ordering, matching, documents and inventory appear across the first two hunts.

GPS arrival, open leads and map overlays are proven by the first location hunt.

Role-specific information and checkpoint synchronization are introduced by the first party hunt.

Complex real-time multiplayer, arbitrary imported code and live AI characters remain outside launch scope.

Each launch hunt should have one memorable signature interaction, not a collection of untested novelties.

## Concept-to-production checklist

Define the player role, central question and intended final realization.

Research and verify the setting or evidence domain.

Draw the investigation graph before drafting every puzzle.

Assign a distinct cognitive job to each branch.

Design the convergence and ending before polishing individual clues.

Specify accessibility and location fallbacks.

Prototype with standard Studio components.

Run logic validation, editorial review and blind playtests.

Field-test every real-world route and record verification.

Publish a fixed candidate only after end-to-end completion.
